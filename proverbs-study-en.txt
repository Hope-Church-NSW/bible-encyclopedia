Introduction to the Book of Proverbs

The central idea of the book: Proverbs is a book of practical wisdom built on the fear of the LORD; it does not deal with wisdom as a set of information, but as a way of life that manifests itself in speech, work, wealth, family, friendship, authority, restraint of passions, and dealings with others. The book begins with the proclamation that “The fear of the LORD is the beginning of knowledge,” then leads the reader on a journey from paternal instruction to personal wisdom, and from warning against ignorance and sin to discovering that true wisdom is bound up with knowing God and walking in His ways.

6
1. Name of the book and its meaning

The name of the book in Arabic is Proverbs, and its singular is mashal.

As for the Hebrew name, it is:

מִשְׁלֵי — Mishlei

This is the plural of the word מָשָׁל — Mashal.

The word does not mean only “a proverb” in the sense we use in didactic stories, but carries a broader range of meanings, such as:

A wise saying.

A brief wisdom saying.

A comparison.

A teaching principle.

A saying that carries a moral or spiritual application.

For this reason, “the Proverbs” are, in reality, compressed sayings of wisdom intended to shape the life of the human being.

2. The Author

Most of the book is associated with King Solomon son of David.

The book says:

“The Proverbs of Solomon son of David, king of Israel.”

But the book itself does not ascribe all of its proverbs to Solomon.

In chapter 22, a group appears associated with:

“The Proverbs of Solomon”

And in chapter 25 we find:

“This also is a proverb of Solomon that the men of Hezekiah king of Judah transcribed.”

There are also sections associated with:

The wise.

The sayings of Agur son of Jakeh.

The sayings of King Lemuel.

Thus the book is a collection of diverse wisdom sayings compiled and edited over a long period of time.

3. Solomon and the Meaning of His Name

The Hebrew name of Solomon:

שְׁלֹמֹה — Shelomoh

It is related to the Hebrew root:

שָׁלוֹם — Shalom

which is associated with the meanings:

Peace.

Wholeness.

Well-being.

completion.

This is very interesting in light of Solomon’s life; his reign was associated with a period of stability and prosperity compared with David’s warrior era.

Solomon was the son of David and Bathsheba, and succeeded David on the throne of Israel.

4. Solomon’s year of birth and death

The Bible does not give a specific year for Solomon’s birth.

But his reign is usually placed in the tenth century BCE.

Historically, it is generally thought that his reign was approximately between:

970–931 BC.

with scholars differing on the details of the chronology.

As for his death, it occurred after a reign lasting about forty years, and he was succeeded by his son Rehoboam.

5. Why Was Wisdom Associated with Solomon?

The First Book of Kings tells us that Solomon asked the LORD for:

an understanding heart.

He did not ask primarily for:

wealth.

Or:

long life.

Or:

the death of his enemies.

Rather, he asked for the ability to distinguish between good and evil and to judge the people.

For this reason Solomon became the symbol of wisdom in Israel.

The Bible also testifies that his wisdom was very great, and that he spoke many wise proverbs and sayings.

6. The Date of Writing Proverbs

It is wrong to say that the whole book of Proverbs was written in one year.

The book contains different collections.

Some wisdom material may have originated in the time of Solomon, that is, the tenth century BCE.

But the process of gathering the proverbs, arranging them, and completing them continued through generations.

Chapter 25 explicitly states that the men of Hezekiah transcribed the proverbs of Solomon, which indicates that the process of collecting and editing wisdom continued long after Solomon.

Therefore, the book may be understood as the fruit of the Israelite wisdom tradition that extended from the era of the united monarchy to later periods.

7. To Whom Was the Book Written?

The book is addressed especially to:

the young man.

the son.

the student of wisdom.

the person who wants to learn how to live.

But its audience is not limited to youth.

For wisdom in chapter 1 says:

“Let the wise man hear and increase in learning, and the discerning man obtain guidance.”

That is, even the wise need further instruction.

Thus Proverbs becomes a book for the child, the youth, the father, the mother, the leader, the worker, and the king.

8. Reasons for Writing the Book

The book itself declares a set of aims.

These include:

To know wisdom and instruction.

To understand the sayings of insight.

To receive instruction in knowledge, justice, righteousness, and integrity.

To give the foolish wisdom.

To give the youth knowledge and discernment.

To teach the person to discern.

And the ultimate goal is not that a person knows more information.

But that he becomes a wiser person in life before God and people.

9. The Structure of Proverbs

The book can be divided into main units:

Proverbs 1–9

Paternal wisdom discourses.

Proverbs 10–24

The proverbs of Solomon and the sayings of the wise.

Proverbs 25–29

The proverbs of Solomon which the men of Hezekiah copied.

Proverbs 30

The sayings of Agur son of Jakeh.

Proverbs 31

The sayings of King Lemuel and the virtue of the excellent woman.

This division is important, because it shows that the book is not a collection of proverbs arranged randomly.

Rather, behind it lie multiple literary and wisdom collections.

10. The Key to the Book — the Fear of the Lord

Among the most important verses of the book:

"The fear of the Lord is the beginning of knowledge, but fools despise wisdom and instruction."

This verse is nearly the theological key to the entire book.

For biblical wisdom is not merely:

Intelligence.

Nor:

Worldly experience.

Nor:

The ability to succeed.

Wisdom begins when a person acknowledges that:

God is the final reference point for truth and goodness.

11. The Difference between Knowledge and Wisdom

The Book of Proverbs presents an important distinction.

Knowledge means to know.

But wisdom means knowing how to use what you know in the right way.

Man can be:

Highly educated

And yet:

Spiritually foolish.

Because the problem is not always a lack of information.

But rather the rejection of the truth that the person knows.

12. The Wise and the Fool

The book continually uses the duality:

The wise ↔ the fool.

But the "fool" in the Book of Proverbs is not necessarily a person of low intelligence.

He may be very intelligent.

But his problem is that he:

Rejects discipline.

Rejects advice.

Trusts in himself.

Does not fear God.

Repeats the mistake despite the warning.

Therefore, folly in Proverbs is a moral and spiritual problem as much as it is an intellectual one.

13. The Beginning of Wisdom

The book says:

"The fear of the Lord is the beginning of wisdom, and the knowledge of the Holy One is understanding."

Here we find a great idea.

Wisdom does not begin from:

What do I want?

Rather it begins from:

What does God want?

For this reason, true wisdom is the reordering of life around God.

14. The Call of Wisdom

In chapter eight, wisdom appears in a poetic personified form.

And she calls out to mankind.

And invites him to:

Understanding.

Knowledge.

Justice.

Truth.

And this literary personification of wisdom has made this chapter very important in theological studies.

But one must be careful in saying that "Wisdom" here is Christ directly in every detail of the text.

It is more accurate to say that the New Testament allows us to see a profound echo between the concept of wisdom in Proverbs and the revelation of Christ as the wisdom of God.

15. Christ and Wisdom

The New Testament declares that Christ is:

"The power of God and the wisdom of God."

This opens an important messianic door in reading the Book of Proverbs.

For wisdom in Proverbs calls man to:

Life.

And warns against:

The way of death.

And in Christ we see the wisdom of God, which appeared in a real historical person, and called mankind to life and salvation.

But one must distinguish between:

The original meaning of the text in its Hebrew context

and

The later Christian application, which reads the text in light of the full revelation of Christ.

16. Wisdom and the Creation of the World

Among the most fascinating passages:

Proverbs 8:22–31.

There, wisdom appears connected to God's work in creation.

And it speaks poetically of her existence before the foundation of the earth.

For this reason, this text has become very important in Christian history when discussing:

Christ.

The Word.

Divine Wisdom.

But from an exegetical perspective, the text must first be studied within wisdom literature itself, where wisdom appears as a literary and poetic figure.

17. Wisdom and the strange woman

The first chapters present a strong warning against:

Adultery.

Sexual enticement.

The strange woman.

This is not merely a minor ethical issue in the book.

Rather, the writer uses the relationship between man and woman symbolically to show the difference between:

The way of life

and

The way of death.

For enticement begins with speech.

Then it turns into desire.

Then into a decision.

Then into bondage.

18. Wisdom and the family

The book is deeply concerned with the household.

We find:

The father.

The mother.

the son.

Discipline.

Upbringing.

Love.

Boundaries.

For the family, in the Book of Proverbs, is not merely a social unit.

It is the first place where wisdom is transmitted from generation to generation.

19. Discipline

The subject of discipline is one of the most important themes of the book.

He says:

"For the one whom the LORD loves, He disciplines."

Discipline here is not merely punishment.

Rather, it carries the meaning of:

Upbringing.

Correction.

Rectification.

Restoring the human being to the right path.

This concept carries over into the New Testament in the understanding of God's relationship with His children.

20. Speech

The Book of Proverbs speaks very extensively about the tongue.

Why?

Because speech reveals the heart.

And the tongue can be:

Medicine.

Or:

A sword.

It can build a person up.

And it can tear him down.

For this reason, the book warns against:

Lying.

Gossip.

Slander.

Babbling.

False witness.

By contrast, it commends:

The fitting word.

The gentle answer.

Truthfulness.

Wisdom in speech.

21. Work and laziness

Among the best-known themes of Proverbs are:

The ant.

The book presents it as a model of diligence.

By contrast, it warns against:

Laziness.

For laziness is not merely inactivity.

Rather, it may turn into:

Poverty.

Neglect.

Disorder.

Lost opportunities.

The book presents hard work as part of wisdom.

22. Money

The Book of Proverbs does not regard money as evil in itself.

But it warns against:

The love of money.

Injustice for the sake of money.

Fraud in trade.

Exploiting the poor.

Reckless guarantees.

Unwise debt.

At the same time, it commends:

Honesty.

Diligence.

Generosity.

Justice.

Giving to the needy.

Thus the issue becomes:

How does a human being use money?

Not:

Is possessing money a sin?

23. Justice

The book is very clear in rejecting injustice.

God cares:

For the poor.

For the destitute.

For the weak.

For the oppressed.

And it warns the king, the judge, and the merchant against using authority, the scales, or the law to achieve personal gain.

And here we see that biblical wisdom is not merely individual.

But it has a clear social and ethical dimension.

24. Friendship

The book says:

A friend loves at all times.

And it emphasizes the importance of:

A faithful friend.

And it warns against:

The company of the wicked.

And this makes clear that a person is greatly shaped by the people whom he allows to enter his life.

And for this reason, friendship in Proverbs becomes a part of character development.

25. Anger

The book warns against:

A quick-tempered person.

And it encourages:

Slowness to anger.

For wisdom is not in the absence of feelings.

But in managing emotions in the right way.

And the wise person does not allow anger to become the master of his decisions.

26. Pride

Among the most famous principles of the book:

Pride goes before destruction, and a haughty spirit before a fall.

Pride in Proverbs is not merely a feeling of pride.

But it is:

Refusal to accept correction.

Excessive self-confidence.

Contempt for others.

Refusal to rely on God.

And therefore pride is connected to a fall.

27. Trust in the Lord with all your heart

One of the best-known texts of the book is:

Trust in the Lord with all your heart, and lean not on your own understanding. In all your ways acknowledge him, and he will direct your paths.

This is not a call to abandon reason.

But a call to not make human understanding the final reference point.

For a person thinks.

And plans.

And learns.

But God remains the one who has sovereignty over the path.

28. Proverbs are not automatic promises

And this is a very important point for the scholar.

Some proverbs are formulated as general principles for life.

But each proverb should not be converted into:

A guaranteed mathematical law in every individual case.

For example:

Train up a child in his way...

It is a principle of wisdom and education.

And it is not an automatic promise that every child trained correctly will never go astray.

And the evidence is that the rest of Scripture presents examples of complex situations.

Therefore Proverbs presents general wisdom, not mechanical equations for life.

29. The Excellent Woman

The book ends with a famous passage:

Proverbs 31:10–31.

And it presents a picture of a woman who combines:

Wisdom.

Work.

Prudent management.

Generosity.

Strength.

Care for her family.

Commerce.

The fear of the Lord.

And the conclusion is very important:

But a woman who fears the Lord, she shall be praised.

For the ultimate value is not in beauty or wealth.

But in:

The fear of the Lord.

30. Agur son of Jakeh

Chapter 30 contains sayings connected with:

Agur son of Jakeh.

And it differs in its style from some sections of the book.

And strong reflections appear in it concerning:

The limits of human knowledge.

The purity of God's word.

Poverty and wealth.

The nature of humanity.

The creatures.

And this confirms again that the book of Proverbs is diverse collections of wisdom and is not composed as a single literary unit.

31. For King Lemuel

Chapter 31 begins:

“The words of King Lemuel, the oracle with which his mother instructed him.”

Here appears the wisdom that a mother transmits to her son, the king.

She warns him against:

Women.

Excessive drinking.

Then she commands him to:

Open his mouth for the mute.

Judge with justice.

Here are brought together:

Personal purity

with:

Social justice.

32. The Most Important Events in the Book of Proverbs

The book is not a book of historical events in the traditional sense.

But within it there are instructive events and scenes.

Among the most prominent are:

The father’s call to the son to pursue wisdom.

The warning of the son against adultery.

Wisdom’s call in the streets.

The conflict between wisdom and folly.

The description of the path of laziness and diligence.

The teachings of Solomon.

The collecting of Solomon’s proverbs by the men of Hezekiah.

The sayings of Agur.

The instructions of Lemuel.

The song of the virtuous woman.

33. The Main Figures in the Book
Solomon

The figure most closely associated with the book.

He represents the wise king who collected and taught the proverbs.

David

He appears as the father of Solomon in the title of the book.

The men of Hezekiah

They had a role in transmitting and collecting a group of Solomon’s proverbs.

Agur

Associated with chapter 30.

Lemuel

A king associated with the sayings of chapter 31.

The mother of Lemuel

She represents the voice of maternal wisdom and wise instruction.

Wisdom

A central literary figure in the first chapters.

Folly

It represents the path that competes with the path of wisdom.

34. The Most Important Message the Book Presents

The message of Proverbs can be summarized as follows:

True wisdom begins with the fear of the LORD and is shown in the way a person lives, speaks, works, and deals with God and people.

Thus the book does not want to make a person:

Only more intelligent.

But rather:

More wise.

35. What Does the Book of Proverbs Point Out to Us?

It indicates that:

Faith is not separate from conduct.

So how do you speak?

And how do you work?

And how do you use money?

And how do you choose your friends?

And how do you deal with your wife or your husband?

And how do you raise your children?

And how do you use authority?

And how do you deal with the poor?

All these matters reveal the kind of wisdom that governs your life.

36. The Christian Dimension in the Book of Proverbs

When we read Proverbs in the light of the New Testament, we see that the wisdom for which the book searches does not find its fulfillment in mere moral rules.

For Christ is revealed in the New Testament as:

The wisdom of God.

And the human being does not only need to know:

What is right?

Rather, he needs:

A new heart that is able to walk in what is right.

Here the book moves from written wisdom to wisdom embodied in Christ.

37. Proverbs and the Gospel

The Book of Proverbs says:

“The fear of the LORD is the beginning of knowledge.”

And the Gospel declares that the knowledge of God reached its climax in Christ.

And Proverbs says:

Walk in the way of life.

And Christ says:

“I am the way, and the truth, and the life.”

Proverbs warns against the way of destruction.

And Christ calls the human being to enter into life.

Thus there is a deep relationship between wisdom literature and the revelation of Christ, without losing the original meaning of each text.

38. Wisdom versus Folly

The central conflict in the book can be summarized as follows:

Wisdom; folly
Fear the Lord; reject God
Accept instruction; reject discipline
Listen to advice; trust yourself
Control your tongue; multiply speech
Work diligently; love laziness
Love truth; use falsehood
Respect others; despise others
Think of the future; live for the moment
Leads to life; leads to destruction
39. Key Conceptual Vocabulary in the Book

There are a group of words that deserve encyclopedic study:

Wisdom — חָכְמָה — ḥokmāh

Understanding — בִּינָה — bīnāh

Knowledge — דַּעַת — daʿat

Fear of the Lord — יִרְאַת יְהוָה

Discipline — מוּסָר — mûsār

Foolishness — אִוֶּלֶת — ʾiwwelet

Evil — רָעָה — rāʿāh

Justice — צֶדֶק — ṣedeq

Righteousness — צְדָקָה — ṣedāqāh

These words reveal that the book possesses an integrated moral and theological system.
