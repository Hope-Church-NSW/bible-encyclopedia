Paul's First Letter to Timothy
1. The Epistle’s Name and the Meaning of the Name

It is known as the First Epistle to Timothy, and it is one of the three Pastoral Epistles: First and Second Timothy and the Epistle to Titus.

The name “Timothy” means “honored by God” or “one who honors God,” and he was one of Paul's closest fellow workers and servants in preaching.

The epistle focuses on sound teaching, the ordering of the church, the selection of ministers, worship, holiness, and the care of God's people.

2. The Author and His Character

The author of the epistle is the Apostle Paul, who describes himself in its opening as an apostle of Jesus Christ by the command of God our Savior and the Lord Jesus Christ, our hope.

Timothy was one of Paul's most prominent fellow workers, his spiritual son, and a participant with him in ministry and evangelism.

In the epistle, Paul appears as an apostle entrusting a new generation of ministers with the responsibility of preserving the faith and serving the church.

3. To Whom Was the Epistle Written?

It was written to Timothy, who was serving in Ephesus.

His mission included confronting strange teaching, ordering the life of the church, overseeing ministers, and teaching believers how to live according to the gospel.

Therefore, the epistle is personal in form, but of great importance for understanding the life and ministry of the church.

4. The Reason for Writing the Epistle

Paul left Timothy in Ephesus to confront certain teachers who were presenting strange teachings and preoccupying themselves with myths, genealogies, and controversies.

Therefore, Paul gives him instructions concerning sound teaching, prayer, worship, the qualifications of bishops and deacons, dealing with widows, the relationships of believers with one another, and the danger of the love of money.

5. Date of the Epistle

The epistle was likely written after the period covered by the Book of Acts, at a later stage of Paul's ministry.

Many studies place it in the early or middle sixties AD, although scholars differ concerning its precise date and place of writing.

6. The Main Theme

The central theme is:

To preserve the true faith and order the household of God so that the church may live a life worthy of the gospel.

For this reason Paul says:

“That you may know how you ought to conduct yourself in the house of God, which is the church of the living God, the pillar and foundation of the truth”
(1 Timothy 3: 15).

7. Sound Teaching

Paul begins by warning Timothy against different teaching.

The church is not called to collect diverse religious ideas, but to preserve the sound teaching that accords with the gospel.

8. The Purpose of the Commandment

Paul declares that the aim of the commandment is:

“Love from a pure heart, a good conscience, and sincere faith”
(1 Timothy 1: 5).

This clarifies that sound doctrine is not an intellectual end separate from life, but must produce love, purity, and genuine faith.

9. The Law and Its Proper Use

Paul speaks of the law, explaining that the law is good if one uses it properly.

The law exposes sin and declares evil, but it must not become a means for empty controversies or self-justification.

10. The Glorious Gospel

Paul places sound teaching within the framework of the gospel:

“According to the gospel of the glory of the blessed God”
(1 Timothy 1: 11).

The final standard for church teaching is the gospel that declares the glory of God.

11. Christ's Mercy in Paul's Life

Paul presents himself as a living example of grace, because before his faith he was a blasphemer, a persecutor, and a violent man.

But he received mercy.

Thus, his life becomes a testimony that the grace of Christ is able to transform even the sinners most resistant to the truth.

12. Christ Came into the World to Save Sinners

Paul says:

“This is a faithful saying and worthy of all acceptance, that Christ Jesus came into the world to save sinners, of whom I am chief”
(1 Timothy 1: 15).

This is one of the greatest evangelical statements in the epistle: Christ came for salvation.

13. Christ as an Example of Longsuffering

Paul sees that his mercy was not only for his salvation, but also became a model of Christ's longsuffering toward those who would believe in him.

Thus, Paul's personal testimony becomes a proclamation of Christ's mercy.

14. Prayer for All

Paul asks that the following be offered:

“Supplications, prayers, intercessions, and thanksgivings for all people”
(1 Timothy 2: 1).

The church bears the world before God in prayer.

15. God Desires the Salvation of All

Paul says:

“Who desires all people to be saved and to come to the knowledge of the truth”
(1 Timothy 2: 4).

Here the universality of the purpose of salvation and the call to proclaim the gospel to all people are evident.

16. The One Mediator

Paul declares:

“For there is one God and one mediator between God and humanity: the man Christ Jesus”
(1 Timothy 2: 5).

Christ is the mediator between God and humanity, and he gave himself as a ransom for all.

17. Prayer and Worship

Paul connects worship with believers' conduct and spiritual disposition, calling for prayer in every place, together with a life worthy of godliness.

Worship is not merely an assembly, but a life before God.

18. Women in the Church

Paul addresses the ordering of worship and the responsibilities of men and women within the congregation.

These texts must be understood within the context of the epistle and the broader teaching of the New Testament, while distinguishing between the principle of holiness and the order of worship and the ecclesiastical applications over which interpretive traditions have differed.

19. Qualifications of the Overseer

The third chapter presents one of the most important texts concerning church leadership.

Therefore, the overseer must be above reproach, the husband of one wife, self-controlled, hospitable, able to teach, and not a lover of money.

The list reveals that the basis of leadership is not giftedness alone, but character, godliness, and maturity.

20. Managing the Household

Paul requires that the leader manage his own household well.

The reason is:

“If anyone does not know how to manage his own household, how will he care for the church of God?”
(1 Timothy 3: 5).

Thus, church leadership is connected with personal responsibility.

21. Deacons

Paul likewise presents the qualifications of deacons, emphasizing the necessity of dignity, sincerity, and freedom from the love of money, after they have first been tested.

Church ministry is a spiritual responsibility, not merely an administrative office.

22. The Church Is the Household of God

Paul describes the church as:

“The church of the living God, the pillar and foundation of the truth.”
(1 Timothy 3: 15).

The church bears witness to the truth in the world and assumes responsibility for preserving and teaching the gospel.

23. The Mystery of Godliness

Paul arrives at a great Christian proclamation:

“Great is the mystery of godliness: God was manifested in the flesh, vindicated in the Spirit, seen by angels, preached among the nations, believed on in the world, received up in glory.”
(1 Timothy 3: 16).

This text brings together, in a concentrated form, the incarnation, preaching, faith, and glorification.

24. Warning against Apostasy

Paul prophesies that some people will depart from the faith and occupy themselves with deceiving spirits and teachings of demons.

This clarifies the danger of false teaching to the church.

25. Rejecting What Contradicts God's Purpose

He warns against teachings that forbid marriage or command abstinence from foods created by God.

Paul responds by affirming that God's creation is good if it is received with thanksgiving.

26. Training in Godliness

He calls Timothy to train himself for godliness.

He says:

“For physical exercise is profitable for little, but godliness is profitable for all things, since it holds promise for the present life and the life to come.”
(1 Timothy 4: 8).

Godliness, therefore, is an eternal investment, not merely a religious appearance.

27. Be an Example to Believers

Paul says to Timothy:

“Let no one despise your youth, but be an example to the believers in word, in conduct, in love, in spirit, in faith, in purity.”
(1 Timothy 4: 12).

The minister preaches first through the word, but he also preaches through his life.

28. Reading, Exhortation, and Teaching

Paul asks Timothy to devote himself to reading, exhortation, and teaching.

This clarifies the centrality of the word of God and biblical teaching in the ministry of the church.

29. Gifts of Ministry

Timothy must not neglect the gift that is in him.

Ministry requires the continual development of gifts together with godliness and teaching.

30. Respect Every Group in the Church

Paul gives instructions for how to deal with older men, young women, young men, and widows.

Here it appears that the church is a spiritual family, and its relationships must be founded on respect, purity, and love.

31. Widows

Paul presents a practical system for caring for widows, distinguishing between those who have relatives able to support them and those who are truly in need of the church's support.

This reveals the church's responsibility toward those in need, together with the necessity of wisdom in managing its resources.

32. Ministers Worthy of Honor

He says:

“Let the elders who rule well be considered worthy of all honor, especially those who labor in the word and teaching.”
(1 Timothy 5: 17).

Teaching and pastoral ministry have great value in the church.

33. Dealing with Accusations against Ministers

Paul establishes precise rules for receiving an accusation against an elder, affirming the necessity of two or three witnesses.

This highlights the principle of justice and of not rushing to judgment.

34. Slaves and Masters

Paul gives instruction to believers living in different social circumstances, calling them to conduct themselves in a manner worthy of the name of God and the teaching.

Thus, work and social relationships are subject to the standard of Christian faith.

35. The Love of Money

Paul arrives at one of his most famous warnings:

“For the love of money is a root of all evils.”
(1 Timothy 6: 10).

The problem is not money itself, but loving it and making it the center of trust and ambition.

36. True Riches

Paul directs the rich not to place their hope in uncertain riches, but in God.

He asks them to be generous and ready to give.

Thus, money becomes an instrument for ministry rather than a master over the heart.

37. “O Man of God”

Paul addresses Timothy:

“But you, O man of God, flee these things and pursue righteousness, godliness, faith, love, patience, and gentleness.”
(1 Timothy 6: 11).

This is a concise portrait of the minister: he flees evil and pursues virtues.

38. The Good Fight of Faith

Paul calls Timothy:

“Fight the good fight of faith; take hold of eternal life.”
(1 Timothy 6: 12).

The Christian life is not passive surrender, but struggle, steadfastness, and holding fast to Christ.

39. The Appearing of Christ

Paul concludes by looking forward to:

“THE APPEARING OF OUR LORD JESUS CHRIST”
(1 Timothy 6: 14).

Christian ministry thus moves between faithfulness in the present and awaiting the appearing of Christ.

40. Christ in First Timothy

Christ is the hope, the Savior, the one mediator between God and humanity, who gave Himself as a ransom, who was manifested in the flesh, preached among the nations, believed on in the world, taken up in glory, and the Lord who will appear at the appointed time.

Accordingly, the church is related to Christ: it preserves His teaching, lives in His godliness, and awaits His appearing.

41. Connections with the Old Testament

The epistle rests on an Old Testament background in its themes of the Law, creation, marriage, food, godliness, poverty and wealth, justice, testimony, and the discipline of the congregation.

The concept of the “man of God,” used in the Old Testament to describe servants of God, also appears and is reapplied here to Timothy as a servant who bears responsibility for proclaiming the truth.

42. The Major Movement of the Epistle

The epistle begins with the commission to Timothy to preserve sound teaching and confront misguided teachers. Paul then presents himself as a witness to the grace of Christ that saved him despite his past. It subsequently turns to the life of the church, speaking of prayer for all people and of Christ as the one mediator and ransom for humanity. It then lays down the foundations of church leadership and the qualifications of bishops and deacons, and declares the church to be the house of God and the pillar and foundation of the truth. From this point, it moves to a warning against apostasy and demonic teaching, and calls Timothy to godliness, exemplary conduct, reading, teaching, and not neglecting his gift. It then regulates relationships within the church, the care of widows, the honoring of servants, and dealings with elders, slaves, and the wealthy, until it reaches its warning against the love of money and its call to fight the good fight of faith. The epistle concludes with the prospect of Christ’s appearing, so that every ordering of the church, every teaching, every practice, and every struggle is connected to a higher goal: that the church may preserve the faith, live in godliness, and await the appearing of the Lord Jesus Christ.

43. Encyclopedic Summary

First Timothy is an epistle concerning the preservation of the faith, the ordering of the church, and the formation of a servant worthy of the house of God. It confronts strange teaching with the love that proceeds from a pure heart, a good conscience, and sincere faith, and sets before the church a model of leadership founded on godliness, maturity, exemplary conduct, and teaching. At its center stands Christ as the one mediator between God and humanity, who gave Himself as a ransom, who was manifested in the flesh, preached among the nations, and taken up in glory. The mission of the church flows from Christ; therefore, the church must be the house of God and the pillar and foundation of the truth. The epistle connects doctrine and conduct, calling the servant to be an example, believers to love and purity, the wealthy to generosity, and all people to prayer and godliness. Finally, it presents the warning against the love of money and apostasy alongside the call to fight the good fight of faith, hold fast to eternal life, and await the Lord’s appearing. Thus, the epistle presents an integrated model of a church that preserves the truth, lives in godliness, shepherds the people of God, serves the world, and awaits the appearing of Christ.
